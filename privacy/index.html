Privacy policy

Last updated November 15, 2026.

The short version

  • We don't sell or share your data, and we don't run ads, analytics or tracking on any of our sites or apps.
  • No tracking cookies, anywhere. The learn site does save your progress in your browser so you can pick up where you left off, but not like those cookies: it never leaves your device, and we can't see it.
  • Domovoi keeps your data on your phone and on your own Domovoi server. Coders Farm never receives it.

Who we are

Coders Farm, Inc. is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit in Lansing, Michigan that teaches people to code for free and makes Domovoi. Questions about privacy go to info@codersfarm.org.

This website

  • No cookies, analytics or tracking. codersfarm.org sets no cookies, stores nothing on your device, and runs no analytics or advertising code.
  • Hosting. Render hosts this site. Like any web host, it receives the standard details of each request (your IP address, your browser, the page you asked for) to serve the page and keep the service secure.
  • Fonts and news images. The page's fonts load from Google Fonts, and two of the news cards show images from Forbes and Business Insider. Those companies receive your IP address and browser details when your browser fetches them.
  • The email list. “Join the email list” opens a Google Form. What you enter (your email address) is stored in Google Forms for us and used only to send Coders Farm updates. Email us to be removed, and we'll delete your address.
  • Donations. Donations are handled by Stripe at donate.codersfarm.org under Stripe's own privacy policy. We receive your name, email address, the amount and the date, so we can send receipts and keep the records a nonprofit must keep. We never see your full card number.
  • Links. GitHub, Discord, X, LinkedIn and Facebook follow their own privacy policies when you visit them.

learn.codersfarm.org

  • No accounts, tracking cookies or analytics. You never sign up or sign in.
  • Your progress stays in your browser. Lesson progress, the code you write, quiz answers and your settings are saved in your browser's local storage, so you can pick up where you left off.
  • Is that a cookie? A cousin of one, but not like those cookies. A cookie is sent to a website every time you visit, which is what lets sites track you. Local storage never leaves your browser: we never see it, nothing reads it but the learn site on your device, and clearing the site's data in your browser deletes it.
  • Hosting. GitHub Pages hosts the learn site and receives the standard details of each request, like any web host.
  • The code editor loads from the jsDelivr content network, which sees your IP address when it sends the editor's files.
  • The narrator uses the voices built into your browser. Some browsers' online voices send the text being read to the browser's maker.

The Domovoi app for Android

Effective October 21, 2026. Published by Coders Farm, the makers of Domovoi (github.com/coders-farm-official/domovoi). Questions: info@codersfarm.org.

Domovoi is a home computer system that runs on your own hardware: your music, videos and files in every room and on your phone, with voice and AI when you want them. This policy covers the Domovoi Android app (com.domovoi.app), from Google Play, from GitHub, or from your own Domovoi server. The short version:

  • Coders Farm collects nothing from the app. There is no account with us, no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting and no tracking of any kind. The app never sends us anything unless you choose to email us a report (below).
  • What the app shares, it shares with your own Domovoi server, on your home network, which you or your household run. We cannot see it.
  • Phone chats run on the phone. A chat with “Domovoi on this phone” is answered by a model on the phone, and what you ask it and what it answers stay on the phone, with two exceptions you choose each time: a lookup you allow sends the words of that lookup to the service that answers it (see Lookups you allow), and a document, a photo of a letter or a draft you choose to hand to your home server goes to that server only (see Documents, letters and drafts).

What stays on the phone

The app keeps these in its private storage on the phone. Other apps can't read it, it is left out of cloud backups and phone-to-phone transfers, and uninstalling the app deletes it.

  • Settings: the address of your Domovoi server, the theme, this phone's name, the person it is “listening as”, the phone bar, and the servers you trusted with their identity keys.
  • The household pairing token, sealed with a key in Android's Keystore that never leaves the phone (but see “save to device” for videos, below).
  • Phone chats: the models (downloaded once, see below), the conversations (one file each, until you delete them; “clear all conversations” deletes every one), with what a lookup found (the titles and addresses of its sources) and the text read from a photo or a letter photo, the documents you attach and the drafts written in them (deleted with the chat), drafts you save to the phone's “Chat documents”, and reply reports you save.
  • Your own search, if you set one for lookups: the SearXNG address or the Brave Search key, sealed with a key in Android's Keystore.
  • Problem reports: crashes and “not responding” closes of the app, kept so you can copy one if you want help. They are never sent anywhere.
  • Timer and reminder alarms this phone mirrors from your server, with a reminder's words, so it can ring when the app is closed.
  • Room mode's room key and settings, when you make the phone a room.
  • Audiobooks you save to the phone and your place in them.

The microphone

The app uses the microphone only for features you start, and Android shows its microphone indicator whenever it is on.

  • Phone chats: the microphone opens only when you tap the mic button, and closes when you stop talking, when you tap again, or when you leave the screen. The sound is turned into text on the phone and then discarded: audio is never saved and never sent anywhere. Only the text of your question is kept, as part of the conversation.
  • Chat with your server, and the intercom (“drop in”): while you hold the mic or are in a call, the sound goes to your Domovoi server to be transcribed or to reach the other room.
  • Room mode (a spare phone or tablet as a room speaker, which you turn on yourself after a screen that explains it): the microphone stays on to hear the wake word, on the phone. Only what is said after the wake word goes to your Domovoi server, like any Domovoi room. A notification that cannot be hidden stays while it listens, and “Stop listening” turns the microphone off.

Voice ID (recognising who in the household is speaking) is a feature of your Domovoi server, not of the app. If your household turned it on, your server may compare the speech it receives from the app with the voice samples stored on that server. Nothing about voices is stored by the app or sent to us.

The camera

The camera is asked for only when you first use it, and you can always say no. Room mode can scan the room code your dashboard shows: the camera opens only when you press “Scan”, the picture is read on the phone and never stored or sent, and you can paste the code instead. In a phone chat you can take a photo to ask about (see below); the photo is held in memory, not saved.

What phone chats read on the phone

A phone chat can answer questions about the phone itself, read on the phone at the moment you ask and never sent anywhere (not to your Domovoi server, not to us): what is playing, the battery, free space, whether you are on Wi-Fi or mobile data, your next alarm, Do Not Disturb, the phone's model and Android version, connected headphones, and the music, videos and saved audiobooks on the phone. What it reads goes only into that conversation, which stays in the app's private storage.

Three things need your permission, asked the first time you ask for them, with the reason, and always optional:

  • Approximate location, for “where am I” (the last location Android already has, shown as coordinates; never looked up online, never in the background). Precise location is asked for separately and only to read the Wi-Fi network's name, which Android shows to no other app.
  • Contacts, to call or text someone by name. Without it, you choose the person in your contacts app each time.
  • Calendar, to read what is on this phone's calendar.

Say no and the phone chat says it can't do that, and doesn't ask again; you can change it in Android's settings for the app. Calls, texts, emails, directions, calendar events, timers, alarms, links and sharing open the app that does them (timers and alarms, the Clock app), after you tap or say yes; you finish there. The app never reads your texts, call log or notifications.

In a phone chat you can ask about a photo, on phones fast enough to read one. The camera is asked for the first time you tap “take a photo”; choosing a photo uses Android's photo picker and needs no permission. The photo is shrunk and read by the model on the phone, in memory: it isn't saved to your gallery or the app's storage, it isn't sent anywhere (except a letter you choose to have your home server read, below), and it is discarded from memory once Domovoi has answered. The chat keeps a “photo” mark, not the photo. Since October 22, 2026 the words read from the photo are kept beside its answer in the phone's own chat storage, so you can ask about it afterwards; they are deleted with the chat.

Lookups you allow

A phone chat can look something up online, but only when you allow it. Before anything goes to the internet, a card shows the exact words (or the page address) and where they would go, and you choose Allow once, Allow in this chat or Not now. “Always allow internet”, in the model settings, is off until you turn it on. Nothing goes to Coders Farm.

  • At home, with your Domovoi server connected, the phone asks your server. A search first looks in the server's own manual and libraries, which stay in your house, with no card. Only if you allowed the lookup, and your server's own internet setting allows it, does the server go on to Wikipedia, Wikidata and the web through the search your household set up, or read the page you named. A server set to keep everything in the house refuses, and the phone does not go around it.
  • Away from home, or when your server can't be reached, the phone itself sends the words to Wikipedia (en.wikipedia.org). If you chose “Use your own search” in the model settings, it sends them to Wikipedia first and then to your own SearXNG address or to Brave Search with your own key. The key is sent only to Brave. Those services see the words, the phone's internet address and the app's name and version, and treat them under their own policies. The phone sends no account, household token or cookie.
  • A page you name (such as “what does this page say”), away from home, is fetched by the phone from that page's own site, with no cookies, and only its text is read (2 MB at most). The site sees the request like any visit. Pages must start with https://.

What comes back is read on the phone. The conversation keeps your question, the answer, the titles and addresses of the sources, and whether the chat may go online. It stays in the app's private storage until you delete the chat.

Documents, letters and drafts

Documents you attach or write in a phone chat stay in the app's private storage, and are deleted with the chat. Three things can go to your own Domovoi server, each only when you tap it:

  • “Ask the home server”, for a PDF this phone can't read itself: the server reads its text in a temporary folder, sends the text back and keeps nothing.
  • “Use home server” on a long summary: the document is deleted on the server as soon as it has been read; its text and the summary are held in the server's memory only until the phone has taken the summary. If the phone never comes back for it, the server forgets it after at most 24 hours (10 minutes once it is done and no phone is watching), and a restart forgets it at once.
  • “A letter for the home server”, on a phone that can't read photos itself: that one photo goes to your home server, is read there in memory and isn't kept there; only the text read from it comes back.

When you ask what a letter says, the text read from the photo is kept beside the answer in that phone chat, so you can ask about it again; it is deleted when you delete the chat. The photo itself is never kept.

Send to home on a draft puts that one file (Word or PDF) into your home server's Documents, in the “From Chat” folder, where it stays like any file there. Nothing else of the chat is sent.

Photos, music, videos and files on the phone

When no server is connected, the app can play the music and videos on the phone and show its photos and the folders you add, with the media permissions Android asks you for. They are read on the phone and not sent anywhere. A file goes to your Domovoi server only when you upload it there yourself.

Music, podcasts and videos you save from your server with “save to device” are handed to Android's download manager and land in the Downloads/Domovoi folder, where other apps with media access can see them; they are not in the app's private storage, and removing the app does not remove them. For a saved video the download manager also keeps your household token in its own private database until you remove that download from Android's list. Music, podcast and audiobook saves carry no token. “Save to phone” for an audiobook stays in the app's private storage.

What goes to your Domovoi server

When you connect the app to a Domovoi server (at an address you give it, or one it finds on your home network by asking the addresses there whether they are a Domovoi server), it talks to that server and to nothing else of ours. It sends what each feature needs: your chat messages and mic audio, what you play and control, files you upload, the documents, letters, drafts and lookups you hand to it, timers you set or cancel, this phone's name, and the pairing token so the server knows the phone. Documents and spreadsheets you edit in the app open in your server's own editor page (the page and its fonts come from the server, and what you type, and the edits of other household devices editing the same document, go to and from the server only). In room mode the phone also sends the room's health to the server: battery level, whether it is charging, Wi-Fi signal and speed, memory, temperature and whether the microphone is working.

Your server is run by you or your household, on your own hardware; what it keeps is described in its own documentation (docs/SECURITY_PRIVACY.md in the Domovoi repository). Coders Farm has no access to it.

Traffic to your server may be plain HTTP: the app allows that only to addresses on your home network (private addresses such as 192.168.x.x, and local names). Every address on the internet must use HTTPS. The same rule holds for your own search address: http:// only for an address on your home network, https:// for anything else.

What goes to the internet

The app contacts the internet only for these, each started by you:

  • Phone chat models and phone voices: downloaded once, when you ask, over HTTPS from Hugging Face (the language model, the speech recognition and voice detection models, and the Piper voices), each file pinned to one exact version and checked against its fingerprint. Hugging Face sees the request like any download (your IP address); nothing about you or your conversations is sent.
  • Lookups you allow (see Lookups you allow): away from home, the words of the lookup go to Wikipedia and, if you set one, your own search, or the address of a page you named goes to that page's site. Never without your allow.
  • Internet radio and other streams your server lists: when you play one, the phone fetches it from the station's own server, like a browser would.
  • Links you open, such as this policy, open in your browser.

The app does not check for updates by itself: Google Play updates it, or your Domovoi server offers new versions.

Reports about Domovoi's replies

If one of Domovoi's answers in the app's Chat is offensive, harmful or wrong, whether the model on the phone or the one on your home server wrote it, long-press it and choose report this reply. The report shows exactly what it holds: your question before that reply, the reply, which model wrote it, where (this phone or the home server) and when, the app's version, and an optional note.

  • Save on this phone keeps it in the app only (offered for the phone's own replies, listed under its model settings).
  • Email draft to Coders Farm opens your email app with the report addressed to info@codersfarm.org (codersfarm@gmail.com in the first release, build 26101501). Nothing is sent until you press send in your email app. If you send it, we receive what the report shows plus what any email carries (your email address), and use it only to look into the reply and improve Domovoi. Ask us at the address you sent it to, or at info@codersfarm.org, to delete it.
  • Share as text hands it to any app you choose.

The Domovoi server

The Domovoi server runs on your own hardware and sends nothing to Coders Farm. Speech recognition, the language models, the voices and the music player all run on that machine, so your voice never leaves your network.

Whether it uses the internet at all is your household's choice when you set it up, and you can change it later. “No, keep everything in the house” closes every internet feature at once; what each feature sends, and how to turn it off, is listed in Will your Domovoi have internet? That setting can't see two things you'd choose yourself: a language model server that isn't on your network, such as a cloud model (Settings → Internet warns about it), and a plugin you install that makes its own connections.

The full security and privacy model is in docs/SECURITY_PRIVACY.md.

Children

Our sites and apps are not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect anything from anyone, of any age. The learn site collects nothing at all; it keeps progress only in your own browser.
